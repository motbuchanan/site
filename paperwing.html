Paper Wing

Pick a plane

Ten planes, one sheet of paper each. Tap one, fold along, then take it outside and log the flight.

Paper: regular letter size (8.5 × 11). Fold on a hard table and run your thumbnail down every crease. Crisp folds fly; soft folds wobble.

Why it flies

Balance: the one rule that matters

Every plane has a weight center (where it balances on your finger) and a lift center (the middle of the wing area). The weight center must sit a little ahead of the lift center.

Test: balance the plane on one finger. The finger should be just in front of the middle of the wings.

Lift and wing loading

Wings shove air down. Air shoves the plane up. More speed or more wing means more lift.

Wing loading = weight ÷ wing area. Same sheet of paper, same weight, so the only thing that changes is how much of it became wing. Small wings (Dart) must fly fast to stay up. Big wings (Glider) can float slowly.

Dihedral: the shallow Y

Looking from the front, the wings should angle up slightly, like a shallow Y. If the plane tips, the low wing gets flatter and makes more lift, which pushes it back level. Flat wings drift; drooping wings roll over.

Fix it

Dives into the groundBend the back edges of both wings up a little (about the width of a fingernail). That is "up elevator."
Climbs, stalls, dropsToo much up. Flatten the back edges, or add a paperclip to the nose.
Turns one wayCheck the wings match. Then bend the back edge of the wing on the turning side up a touch.
Rolls overRaise the wings into more of a Y (more dihedral).
WobblesSoft folds or gaps. Re-crease everything hard. Tape the body shut behind the nose.
Just fallsThrow level and medium, not hard. Point the nose slightly down for a Dart, level for a Glider.
