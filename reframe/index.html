Talk to a smart, anxious listener
The seven habits behind every suggestion.
1. Short and plain
Short sentences. Plain English even for complex ideas. If it needs a second read, cut it.
2. "You" for their action, plain names for the rest
"You" for what they will do. Name who does everything else. Never "the patient", "the user", "the client".
3. No blame framing
Attach the problem to the situation and the goal, not the person. Not "your son's deformity." Not "user error."
4. De-jargon by default
Lead with the plain meaning. The technical term is optional and never the opener.
5. Honest, pointed at the next step
Every outcome truthful and forward-facing. Never sugarcoat, never fatalistic.
6. End on an action
Answer "what do I do now": next visit, what to watch for, who to call.
7. Center the person, not the problem
Lead with the person and what becomes possible, not what is wrong.
