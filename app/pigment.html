Pigment

A giant color wheel to find and name colors, mix them like paint or light, play color games, and keep your own.

Why I made it

Pigment was a teaching tool for my son. We were learning about color mixing and color theory, and I wanted a fun way for him to get the ideas through games. Then he started inventing names for colors and hunting for new ones, so I gave him a huge spectrum of real, well-named colors from crayons, paints and art supplies, and a place to mix his own and name them. Making it a hunt he could get lost in was the first phase of development beyond the lessons.

How it came together

463 named colors sit in a compact block parsed at runtime, tagged by source: crayon, artist pigment, paint and products, nature and food, old world, web standard. Naming finds the nearest color and invents a name when nothing is close enough, so the same color always gets the same made-up name. Paint mixing runs in red-yellow-blue space with a strength correction, which is the step that makes yellow plus blue come out green instead of olive. Light mixes additively, so red plus green light makes yellow. A kid-facing design pass added a paint jar that fills as you mix, confetti on discoveries, and a silly-name dice.

What I learned

Straight averaging of paint colors drains them. The fix was small, but without it the mixer was wrong in a way a kid would notice right away. Also, older phones render some newer emoji as blank boxes, so I avoided them.
