The Keep

A living valley drawn in letters. Walk it, meet the people who live there, then take the road out to the next one.

Walk the KeepSee it on the phone

Why I made it

I saw a clip of a tiny 3D world made of nothing but text and wanted to see if I could grow one that was actually alive. Not a game with a score, and not a building sandbox. A place you walk around in, where the people have lives of their own. I keep growing it in the open instead of ever calling it done, and it is turning into a world to travel: valley after valley, each with its own people, weather and history. Next comes a story that starts in your own home valley.

How it came together

Every valley grows from one seed number: the land, the keep, the farms, eight people with names, families, routines and things to say, even its weather and its week of news. It started as one ray per screen column drawn in letters, and now there are six ways to see the same world, four of them on the graphics chip. Each version goes through a tester that tries to break it and a separate reviewer before it ships. It opens on a hearth at sunrise, drawn in the same letters as the world.

What I learned

How far you can get with almost nothing, and how much a world needs people in it. Teaching the folk to talk taught me more than the engine did: a line only works if it is true in that valley, right now.
