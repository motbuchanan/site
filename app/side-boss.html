Why I made it

I built a gamified back office for a friend who does leatherwork, pen making and knife making. When it was done I realized that if I stripped out the craft-specific goals, it would work for anyone starting a side hustle or trying to track the work they already do in a way that is actually fun. So I forked it into a free tool: the quest engine kept, the leather stripped out. It fits candles, prints, a booth at a fair or an online shop. Free, on your phone, nothing to sign up for.

How it came together

One HTML file, everything saved on your device. Character creation captures your hustle, then five seasons of quests walk you from getting ready through first sales, finding your people, the busy season and leveling up. A Ledger logs sales and costs and charts your growth, and logging your first sale finishes a quest on its own. Seven boss battles trigger off what you actually do: first sale, a hundred-dollar day, a thousand in revenue. If you sell in person, a Shows tab weighs each market's booth fee against what you made there. It installs to your home screen and updates itself.

What I learned

The tracker should feed the game, not sit beside it. Making a logged sale finish a quest and trip a boss is the moment it stopped feeling like two apps bolted together.
