Civic Compass

A pocket civic toolkit: election dates with reminders, who represents you from city hall to Congress, a voting plan, and tools to help you canvass or organize. Nonpartisan, and nothing leaves your phone.

Why I made it

I built the full version for a local volunteer group that was juggling four or five apps to keep their people informed: one for the calendar, one for contact lists, another for canvassing. I wanted all of it folded into one thing you keep on your home screen. This is the white-label version, stripped of any one group's branding and filled with real Summit County officials, so anyone can see what the tool does regardless of who they vote for.

How it came together

One HTML file, everything saved on your device, works offline. It layers by how deep you want to go: open it to check a date, tap a representative to get their office, or build a voting plan and step into the canvassing tools if you are the type who wants to knock on doors. The officials and dates are real and carry a confirm-at-your-Board-of-Elections line, because a civic tool that is confidently wrong is worse than none. The same engine can wear any group's name and colors.

What I learned

The hard part was staying neutral without going generic. It had to be useful to someone registering for the first time and to someone running a canvass, in the same few taps, without ever telling them who to vote for. Showing current officeholders instead of a candidate ballot was the move: it is real, it stays accurate between elections, and it belongs to no party.
