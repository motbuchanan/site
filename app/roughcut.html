RoughCut

A real video editor that runs in your phone browser. Import clips, trim and reorder on a touch timeline, lay music and titles under them, add transitions (dip, crossfade, slide, wipe, zoom), animate text in real fonts with an outline or a glow, and export a finished MP4. Nothing uploads. The whole edit and the encode happen on your device, on Android and iPhone alike.

Why I made it

I do all of my video editing on my phone, mostly in CapCut and sometimes in the built-in Samsung editor, and I was always jumping between apps to get one job done. I wanted to build an editor for the way I actually work, with the features I kept hopping around for in one place. Every free app also either stamps a watermark on the export, pushes a subscription, or ships your footage off to a server, so I wanted to prove a phone browser can do the real work end to end with nothing leaving the device. It is also the companion to a video editing course I am building.

How it came together

Plain web, no framework. It decodes and re-encodes real H.264 with WebCodecs and Mediabunny, keeps clips in the browser's private file store, and streams the finished MP4 straight to disk so a long export does not run the phone out of memory. Transitions decode both clips across the cut, and text, fonts and effects are drawn on one canvas that the preview and the export share, so what you see is what you get. On iPhone, where Safari writes a broken audio track, it hands just the audio to a small ffmpeg engine that runs on the phone so the sound still comes out right.

What I learned

Old phones have a hard ceiling. My 2020 phone encodes 720p all day but crashes at 1080p or 30fps, and that is the hardware, not the code. So the real fix was not a faster encoder, it was streaming to disk and defaulting to the setting that always finishes. Making the export honest about what a device can do mattered more than chasing the biggest number. The other lesson came from the iPhone: "works in the browser" really means "works in each browser." The same export that was clean on Android came out silent on the iPhone because Safari handles audio differently, and the honest fix was to carry a small backup engine for that one case instead of pretending one path fits every phone.
