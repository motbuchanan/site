Why I made it

I built it for my son, to go alongside his first-grade schoolwork. I wanted school subjects to feel like something you play instead of a worksheet, so every band member turns one concept into a mini-game. He likes it enough that he asked me to keep building it as he moves up through the grades, so it grows with him.

How it came together

The first version was honestly just math word problems with a music theme painted on top, and you could feel it. I tore it down and rebuilt it around actually making music: each band member has their own mini-games, you recruit rival bands by beating them, and getting better at a subject visibly levels up your band. It is past a hundred versions now, with a huge cast of characters, and it has grown from math and music into spelling, reading, vocabulary, science and geography, with more subjects planned.

What I learned

A theme is not a mechanic. Dressing quizzes up as music did not make it a music game. The fix was making the math be the playing: split a beat and you are doing fractions, count the meter and you are reading a clock. I aimed the difficulty at about an 85 percent success rate on purpose, because that is where learning sticks. And what started as a supplement for one kid working ahead of his grade is rounding out into a real teaching tool.
