The Brew Deck

A homebrew training system. Read recipes whose strength, bitterness and colour are calculated live from the ingredients, play a brew day as a series of decisions and watch each choice move the beer, then build your own recipe from what you have on hand and get it graded against the style.

Why I made it

I do not brew, and I do not drink beer. A friend who is very good at homebrewing was joining a club, and this was one of the very first training tools I tried to build. It became the moment I realised that if I gather accurate information from enough reputable sources, I can build a real teaching tool for something I do not do myself. People would actually brew from it, so getting it right mattered more than anything.

How it came together

Plain single-file web, no framework. At the centre is a small brewing-math engine built from the published formulas, Tinseth for bitterness and Morey for colour, checked against worked examples. Every recipe's numbers are computed from its ingredient list the moment you open it, so a recipe and its stats can never disagree. Around that sit the brew-day decision sims, a batch tracker with a bottling safety gate, an off-flavour trainer, and the build-your-own workshop that grades what you enter.

What I learned

I know nothing about brewing, so I could not be the one who decided it was right. I pulled the numbers and methods from reputable public sources, then had experienced homebrewers look it over, and their encouragement is what kept me building. The last step was making the app check itself, so every instruction it shows is verified against the recipe it came from. A confident wrong number is the real danger when a stranger is going to brew from what you wrote.
