Pocket Shark

A 3D pool table you can walk around, with a shark that reads the table and shows you the best shot. 8-ball, 9-ball, a story-mode hustle up the ladder, and Shark Eyes for real tables.

Why I made it

I wanted a pool game that was a real showpiece, full physics and a camera you can take anywhere, but the part I actually cared about was a shark on your shoulder that tells you the best shot and is not just guessing. Then I pushed it further: a tool that looks at a photo of a real table and finds your shots, and a story mode where you hustle your way up from a basement practice table to a tournament arena.

How it came together

One HTML page, three.js the only dependency. The table runs a physics model I wrote from scratch at 500 steps a second, so the balls take english, throw each other off line and rattle in the jaws. The shark plays every candidate shot, direct, bank, kick and combo, forward on a copy of the table and ranks them by how much aiming error each one forgives and where the cue ball stops. Shark Eyes lifts a real table off a photo with a four-corner homography and runs the same solver. Story mode adds a bankroll, a buy-in at every table, and six house players with their own portraits, and the basement you practice in gets fixed up with what you win. It installs to your home screen and plays offline.

What I learned

A solver that is honest is worth more than one that is confident. Off the break the shark will tell you nothing drops and to play the safe instead, and that turned out to be the feature. The hardest part was making the shot finder fast enough to run on a phone between taps, so it chases the miss to zero by bisection and time-slices the search across frames.
