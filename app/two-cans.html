Two Cans

A private line between two phones. Text, photos, voice notes, doodles and eight turn-based games, locked so only two people can ever get in.

Built for my son and me

This copy is the practice version: pick a side, any password works, and nothing leaves your phone.

Why I made it

I wanted a way to message my son on his iPad that was just the two of us, with nobody else able to reach him. Everything in the app store wanted accounts, contact lists, ads, or a whole friend list, and none of it fit that shape. So I built my own line, the way you would run a string between two tin cans.

How it came together

The hard part was the plumbing. I taught myself Firebase for this one: real logins, a live database, and security rules that only let two specific accounts read or send a single thing. Once two phones could talk in real time, I realized the line could carry a lot more than text. I gave it a drawing pad using the brushes from Nazca, then brought over the best two-player games from my Game Shelf, rebuilt as turn-based so a game can sit mid-move until the other person picks it back up. Chess runs on the same engine that passes the standard move-generator tests. The app itself is one HTML file.

What I learned

The hardest lessons came from his iPad, not my phone. Older Safari crashed on load if the app touched the browser's offline database, so Two Cans keeps everything in memory and still syncs live. And every time I added a new kind of message, like a voice clip, the security rules had to be taught about it too, or the database quietly refused it. That is the lock working: it says no to anything it was never told about.
