Light Scout

A sun planner for photos. Tap a spot on the map, pick a day and a time, and see where the sun will be, which way the shadows fall and when golden hour starts. Plan a shot lines the sun up with a gap between buildings and lists every evening it happens.

Scout the lightSee it on the phone

Why I made it

I take a lot of photos and kept guessing where the light would be. I wanted to look at a building, picture it backlit by a sunset, and know which evening to show up and where to stand. The apps that do this are powerful, but the most common complaint in their reviews is that they are confusing, so I built this one for someone who has never heard the words golden hour.

How it came together

One page. The sun math runs on your phone from the date, the clock and the map, with no sun service, no account and nothing sent anywhere. I checked it against a professional astronomy library and it lands within a few hundredths of a degree, with sunrise and sunset within seconds. Plan a shot walks the next twelve months to find every day the sun sits on your line; point it down 42nd Street in Manhattan and it finds Manhattanhenge by itself. A little compass guide walks you through it the first time and explains any screen when you tap it.

What I learned

The math was the easy part. Time zones took longer: a spot far from home shows times on your phone's clock unless you say otherwise, so every time names its zone, and the two days a year the clocks change are 23 and 25 hours long. The street map I started with went dark the week its provider began requiring a key, and that taught me to keep anything a page borrows close to home.
