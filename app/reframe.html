Why I made it

A colleague was overseeing several young professionals in a medical field, fresh out of school, and they were struggling to be understood. They used precise terminology and complex explanations, and they were accurate, so they could not see the problem. But the average person could not follow them, and it was causing confusion. He asked if I could help with formatting and terminology. As I researched the field and its specialties, it grew to cover other professions too. I wanted a real tool people could adopt, not a portfolio demo.

How it came together

It is fully on-device: no AI, no API, no network calls. It flags and suggests, and you stay the author. Only unambiguous jargon swaps can be applied with one tap. The public fork was built Aug 17 with medical, IT and legal packs, then grew to eight: insurance and billing, veterinary, auto repair, dental, and skilled trades. Every glossary entry is sourced from the profession's own published plain-language material, and a term only auto-swaps when two independent sources agree. Packs are JSON, so you can import, edit and export your own. It also auto-detects which pack a pasted text belongs to.

What I learned

I did not have practitioners to vet every field, so I built the pipeline around published consumer glossaries and vetted the plain-language half myself. A report link in the footer turns early users into the vetters.
