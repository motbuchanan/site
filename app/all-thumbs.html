Why I made it

I can get an AI to build almost anything I can picture, but I did not go to school for any of this. I do not know the correct terminology, and I kept finding gaps in the knowledge behind skills I was already using every day. All Thumbs started as my own curriculum: teach myself what is really happening under my own apps, so I am not just building with AI but understanding what I am doing. Then it turned into a course for anyone else who wants to try building this way.

How it came together

It started Aug 4 as one file called Field Guide, became The Reveal, then became All Thumbs with a cartoon thumb mascot. It is now a hub plus one HTML file per chapter, all ten planned chapters built: computer, internet, browser, Linux, Python, git, hosting, where data lives, what an API call is, and the keystone, my real Game Shelf taken apart from its live repo. Every chapter has working sandboxes, missions, a practice gym with randomized drills, and a five-question quiz. Every gym has two tracks, a general one anyone can pass and The Trail, anchored to my real one-thumb phone builds.

What I learned

I am no expert and I do not claim to be one. This is me doing my best to learn what I do not know, out in the open. It will keep growing every time I hit a new gap: several extensions are already planned from workflows I use right now that just need to be written up. Also, names are hard. I renamed this thing three times before it settled, and a teaching piece with nothing to poke at is not worth shipping.
