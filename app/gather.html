Why I made it

It grew out of the player profiles on the family Game Shelf. My son was having fun making profiles for every family member on his tablet and asking his cousins what their favorite colors were, so I kept adding fields because it delighted him: favorite food, favorite sport, birthday. It also nudged him to go talk to people and ask them questions. Then a conversation at a family gathering about planning the next holiday gave me the idea for a tool that organizes the whole thing. My habit of making everything a little bit fun is why it has themed party games. Before building, I read the one-star reviews of the family gift apps already out there: subscriptions that locked people out of their own lists, ads, the whole family forced to make accounts, logins that broke every December. So I built the opposite. One person runs it, nothing leaves the device, and nobody else has to sign up for anything.

How it came together

One HTML file with everything saved in the browser on your device. The household is the spine: add a person once (birthday, sizes, allergies, favorite treat, wishlist) and every other part pulls from it. Each occasion is its own container with its own theme, gifts, budget, potluck and draw, and it can dress your people up in its own faces. The Secret Santa draw never pairs anyone with themselves, keeps couples apart, and remembers past years so nobody gets the same person twice; I checked it against 4,000 random draws. The reveal is pass-the-phone: press and hold to light the lantern, and only that person sees their match.

What I learned

The best research wasn't an article, it was other apps' one-star reviews. Every fix I wanted was sitting in somebody's complaint. And my first version let every theme share one gift list, so planning Christmas quietly rewrote Halloween. Making each occasion its own container was the real fix, not a patch.
