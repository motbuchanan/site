Why I made it

It started with a conversation about artists who draw pictures you can keep zooming into, with whole other scenes hidden inside, each one telling more of a story. I wanted to build something that could do that. I also wanted one app a kid could have fun with and an artist could actually use, with one UI that has range instead of a kid mode and a pro mode. The name comes from the ground drawings that only make sense zoomed out.

How it came together

Built in phases over about a week starting Sep 5. Tiled infinite canvas with pan, zoom and rotate, a stabilizer, layers, autosave and project files. Then deep-zoom tiles with a depth gauge. Once the zoom worked, I realized the camera tools I already knew from video, keyframes and set movement, would help tell the story, so a Tour panel lets you set keyframes and fly between them. That pushed me to learn more detailed and complex brushes and tools: a brush engine, fun brushes like crayon, rainbow, glitter and stickers, alive tools like a train track with a running train, replay and timelapse video export, symmetry, portals to rooms at depth, a select tool, reference and trace images, a walking sticker and a grow brush. It opens with a nine-slide animated intro you can skip.

What I learned

The first guide I wrote was a wall of text and got rejected in one line. A skippable animated intro sells the idea better. Also, an eraser at max size over three layers crashed my phone, so undo snapshots are now cropped and compressed in the background.
