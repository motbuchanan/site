Where Data Lives

Why your progress is on this phone and nowhere else, and how to fix that.

what data even is

Data is just the stuff your app has to remember

A high score. A checklist someone ticked. Which level they reached. The toast message with their name in it. All of that is data: facts your app wants to still know next time it runs.

Here is the thing nobody tells you at the start. When your app remembers something, it remembers it somewhere, and that somewhere decides who else can ever see it. Pick the wrong somewhere and your kid opens the same game on his iPad to a blank slate, with none of the progress he had on your phone. That is not a bug. That is a storage choice you made without knowing you made it.

This chapter is the one that explains the thing you have actually been fighting: progress that lives on one device and refuses to show up on another.

three places

A web app can keep data in three places

From most forgetful to most permanent:

1. In a variable (memory). A number the page holds while it is open. Close the tab and it is gone, instantly and completely. Good for a score during one game. Useless the moment they leave.

2. In localStorage (this one browser). The browser hands every site a little drawer it keeps between visits. Save the score there and it is still there tomorrow. But that drawer belongs to this browser on this one device. Another phone, another browser, even the same phone in a private tab, gets a different, empty drawer.

3. In a database (the cloud). A cabinet that lives on someone else's always-on computer. Your app sends the data up and pulls it back down. Because it is not on any one device, every device that signs in sees the same thing.

the wall you hit

localStorage is a drawer, not a shared cabinet

localStorage feels like real saving, because it survives closing the app. That is why it fools everyone. It is perfect for a solo app: a drawing pad, a single-player game, a notes tool for yourself.

It hits a wall the instant two people, or two devices, need the same data. Your phone's drawer and the iPad's drawer are separate drawers in separate rooms. Nothing you save in one appears in the other. There is no secret link between them, because localStorage was never meant to leave the device.

The question that tells you which to use

Ask: does anyone on another device need to see this? No, it is just for me on this phone, localStorage is fine and free. Yes, a family high score, a shared checklist, two players, you need the cloud. That one question saves you a rebuild.

the shared cabinet

A database is just a drawer everyone can reach

A database is storage that does not live on any player's device. It lives on a computer that stays on all the time (a server), reachable over the internet. Your app sends data up to it and asks for data back from it. Because every device talks to the same cabinet, they all see the same contents.

Firebase is one of these, made by Google, with a free tier that is plenty for a family app. People say "Firebase" or "Firestore" the way they say "GitHub Pages" for hosting: a specific free service that does the general job. It hands your app two things: a place to store data (the cabinet) and a way to say who is who (sign-in), so the right person gets the right drawer inside it.

You do not have to run the server, patch it, or keep it awake. That is the whole point of a service like Firebase: the always-on computer is someone else's problem. You just send and receive.

Public config is not a password

A Firebase app carries a config block right in the page, and it looks like a secret. It is not. It only names which cabinet to talk to. What actually protects the data is the rules you set on the cabinet, deciding who may read and write. Lock the rules, not the config.

sync

Sync is both devices watching the same drawer

Here is the magic that makes a cloud app feel alive. Each device does not just read the cabinet once. It subscribes: it asks the database to tell it the moment anything changes. So when your phone writes a new high score, the iPad is listening, and its screen updates on its own, within a second, with nobody refreshing anything.

That is a live listener. Your phone writes, the cabinet changes, every subscribed device hears about it and redraws. It is the difference between a photo and a video call. Flip the cloud on below and watch the save on one phone appear on the other by itself.
