# Light Scout: ROADMAP

Self-documenting record of this app. Fetch this file (raw) before any work on Light Scout and check it against what is actually in the folder. Update it with every upload and ship it in the same zip as the change it documents.

## What this is
A sun planner for photos, as one HTML file: tap a spot on a map, pick a day and time, and see where the sun is, how high, which way shadows fall, golden and blue hour, and which faces of a building get light. Plan a shot mode lines the sun up with a gap or a landmark and lists the dates and times in the next 12 months when it will sit there. All sun math runs in the browser (no sun service). Lives at motbuchanan.com/lightscout/ in the motbuchanan/site repo.

## Locked decisions
- One file, no build step, no account, no tracking. The sun math is the app; the map is a picture under it.
- Plain words first, numbers second. Every readout has a sentence a stranger can act on.
- Golden hour = sun between -4 and +6 degrees; blue hour = -6 to -4; sunrise/sunset at -0.833 (center of the sun, with refraction). Stated in the math panel; do not silently change.
- Directions are true north, as the map is drawn. The key says a phone compass can read a few degrees off.
- The tool cannot see terrain, trees or buildings. Say so wherever a height or a time could mislead; the roofline option is the honest manual version of that.
- Times are shown in a named zone, never "local" without saying which. Default is the phone's zone; the picker and the warning exist because this is the number one confusion in every competing app.
- First run shows the three-things card, never a modal; a shared link skips it. Help is the only way to the sheet.
- Plan a shot asks for the look of the sun in the picture, in words and small pictures; the degree bands behind them (low under 10, mid 10 to 25, high over 25, roof = below the roofline angle) stay as they were.
- Place search is on submit only (no autocomplete) and shows "Search results from OpenStreetMap (Nominatim)". Those are policy requirements, not style.
- Street tiles: OpenStreetMap's own servers, visible attribution, browser Referer, normal caching. Satellite: Esri's older open World Imagery endpoint with its attribution; if it ever stops serving, the supported path is a free Esri developer key (2 million tiles a month) and a URL change, or drop the layer.
- Version stamp in two places that must match: meta build + the mb-ver button text (and the toast). Bump both every change, with the real date.

## Open queue (ideas, in rough order of value)
1. Wire onto the site home screen: Tools folder tile, data-game="lightscout/", card copy in Mot's voice (verb "Scout the light"), shot-lightscout-1.jpg and -2 from real phone screenshots, build log entry, index version bump.
2. Moon: rise, set, phase and position (the most asked-for thing in every competitor review). The math is a known model (suncalc has it); one more path on the dial.
3. Save a stamped picture of the plan (PNG with date, time, spot, direction) for people who share screenshots. Needs the map drawn to a canvas, which cross-origin tiles block; draw the dial and the words only, or use a tile source that allows it.
4. Sun at the same time across the year (the whole-year arc fan SunCalc draws), and solstice / equinox jump buttons.
5. Terrain horizon: a manual "the hill toward the sunset is about N degrees high" setting that moves sunrise/sunset, the way the roofline moves the plan. Real elevation data would need a tile service.
6. Installable: manifest + service worker in this folder (cache name must bump with the version; delete only lightscout-* caches). Only worth it if people ask to install.
7. Twilight types (civil, nautical, astronomical) in the day list for the astro crowd.
8. Multiple saved spots.
