# motbuchanan.com: objective site audit

Date: Sunday, October 18, 2026
Source reviewed: repo motbuchanan/site at commit 83e1fc3 (Oct 11), index v7.49 Oct 10, rendered headless at phone (390x844) and laptop (1440x900) sizes, every card's copy read, plus a research round on comparable sites and on how strangers read AI-built work.

## 1. Bottom line

1. The thing you are getting right is the thing most comparable sites fake: everything is real, measured, yours, and in your voice. Keep all of it.
2. The site has a depth problem, not a category problem. 26 tiles get identical weight. A WebCodecs video editor and a Brick Breaker clone are the same 80px square. Your strongest technical piece (Pocket Shark) is inside a folder with its NEW badge hidden.
3. Every door leads with the method ("by asking AI the right questions") and the outcome comes second. The research says that ordering is the one framing strangers penalize, even at equal quality. The fix is a reorder, not a retreat.
4. On a laptop, 70 percent of the screen is empty studio. Plenty of visitors sit at laptops. The desktop experience is a small phone in the dark.
5. A video producer's site contains zero video. Nothing moves except the grid backdrop and the taglines.
6. Housekeeping has drifted again (About version 7.47 vs 7.49, ROADMAP three weeks behind, four orphan pages, a stale "31 KB" brag, a brown evening tint that breaks your own no-warm-background rule).

## 2. What is working (do not touch)

- Real art everywhere. 26 icons you generated or drew, your actual aperture mark, no stock, no emoji-as-icon. This alone separates it from the template OS-portfolio genre.
- Honest, measured stats on cards (live file size, file count). "Honesty is the flex" is carried through: status lines like "Tier 1 complete, more tiers planned" and "I am no expert."
- Card copy in your voice. The why / how / learned structure is exactly what the vibe-coded-portfolio crowd recommends (what broke, what you learned, which decisions the AI could not make). Most people's are empty; yours have specifics (270/270, 463 colors, 1,038 answers, 4,000 draws, 500 steps a second).
- Real recipients. Brew Deck vetted by homebrewers, Reframe for a colleague's team, LiftSafe for coworkers getting certified, Side Boss for a leatherworker, Measure Up for your son, The Upcard because a friend kept playing blackjack. Every portfolio guide says "get one project in front of real users." You have eight.
- The lock screen is a good hook: time, mark, name, one line, swipe up. It is the clearest seven-second moment on the site.
- The shared chrome (CHROME.md) is real craft. One maker is visible across 26 apps that each keep their own world.
- The Game Shelf page is the best single screen on the site. It is a place, not a grid.
- Privacy stance, no tracking, works offline, free, no catch. Say it once more on the front door.
- Humor is present and is yours. The settings jokes, the languages, the airplane toast. Keep the layer.

## 3. What a stranger hits, in order

Strangers scan in a known order: name and role, strongest project, problems you solve, your level of involvement, then hierarchy and a next step. Here is the walk.

1. Lock screen: name, yes. Role: "Designer and maker" is vague, and the line that follows is the method. Seven seconds spent on how, not on what or for whom.
2. Home: 12 equal squares, a rotating joke, no signal about which of these matters. START HERE points at All Thumbs, a beginner course on computer basics whose own card opens with "I am no expert." For a learner that is right. For someone judging you, the first door you push them through is the least showcase-shaped thing you own.
3. Tap an icon: card. Good. But for The Keep the chain is tile, card (why/how/learned), verb button, then the app's own about panel (why/learned again), then the world. Three screens of explanation before the thing. Neal.fun's rule: one verb, one tap, you are doing it.
4. Screenshots: several show title screens and dialogs, not the thing in use. Knight School's two shots are the menu and the "Who's playing" box. Nazca's are the intro slide and the welcome dialog. The research names "show the real product" as the single fastest fix for the AI-site look.
5. Involvement: "Knowing what to ask, and what to throw away, is the craft" is true but abstract. Nowhere does the site say what you do: product decisions, art direction, testing on glass, the rejected solvers, the icons, the copy, the camera-language steering. Readers reward AI skill and distrust AI authorship. The way through is stating your role, not hiding the tool.
6. Next step: a label-free mail icon. No LinkedIn (it was on the old about.html, which is now orphaned). No GitHub link anywhere, even though the repo is public and your stated ethic is "take it, copy it."
7. Desktop: the phone floats in a dark studio with a light shaft. Nothing in the dead space explains what you are looking at or who made it.

## 4. What is missing, ranked by leverage

### A. Hierarchy inside the phone (the biggest single move)
Real phones have widgets. Yours has one (the wordmark). Give the flagship a 2x2 or 2x1 widget in row 1: a live mini-preview or a five-second loop of the app, a one-line promise, the verb. Tap opens its card. This is the "In my hand right now" live stage you liked in September, folded into the shell you decided to keep, with no new front door needed. It also gives you the two-slot FEATURED / WORKING idea for free: the widget is what is in your hand this week; you swap it in one line.

Layout math: 1 widget (2 cells) + 6 loose + 4 folders = 12 cells, one page, layout law mostly intact. Your call which six.

### B. Copy order: outcome, for whom, then method
Lock screen now: "Designer and maker. I build things on a phone by asking AI the right questions."
Two options in your voice, method last:
- "I make games, tools and lessons for the people around me. Every one of them built on a phone, with an AI I taught myself to steer."
- "Games for my son. Tools for the people I work with. A course for anyone who wants to try. All of it built on this phone."
About page: paragraph 2 ("I like solving other people's puzzles...") becomes paragraph 1. "This whole site is a phone" second. The how third.
Taglines: 34 of 39 are good. Five read as apology or burnout and the research says they cost you with strangers: bad sleep, coffee and panic, sleep schedule, "which is worse, apparently," "I don't know what this is yet." Also "spite and coffee" appears twice in Settings. Keep the confident-funny ones (no mercy, airplane mode, just vibes, "the catch is I'm showing off").

### C. Motion and video
Per-app previews: 5 to 8 second muted loops of each app in use, cut in RoughCut, autoplaying in the card gallery like App Store previews. That is proof of two skills at once, and the caption writes itself: "cut in the editor it previews." A 20-second site trailer is optional. This is also the "personal work" you said you miss: it is your real profession and it is absent from your own site.

### D. Source links
Every card gets "see the code" pointing at the public file on GitHub (derived from data-game, one line of JS). External apps point at their repos. This makes the academy ethic real instead of stated, and public repos with real commit history are how strangers see how you worked. LinkedIn goes back into About.

### E. Desktop
Cheap version: on wide screens, open the card as a panel beside the phone instead of over it, and add one line under the phone: "A real phone home screen. Every app on it is one I built. Click any icon." Better version: a quiet side column that only exists on desktop (name, one line, four flagships, say hi, LinkedIn, GitHub). Phone visitors never see it; laptop visitors get the seven-second path.

### F. The ledger
18 of 26 apps are inside folders. There is no place that lists everything. Settings > Storage already counts by category; make it list every app with its size (tap opens the card). That is the plain ledger from the September plan, inside the metaphor, zero new pages.

### G. Involvement line
One sentence per card, or one site-wide line in About: what you decide, what you make, what you test, what you throw away. Make the involvement checkable: "I generated and cropped all 26 icons, wrote every card, tested every build on this phone, and rejected two shot solvers that were confident and wrong."

## 5. The app balance read

By category: 10 play (plus 24 games inside the Game Shelf), 6 learn, 6 tool, 4 make. The featured 8 are deliberately 2/2/2/2, so the home screen is balanced. The raw count still says "game guy" because the Play folder is a monster.

The real imbalance is depth:
- Flagship tier (serious engineering or a real product): Pocket Shark, RoughCut, Nazca, The Keep, Knight School engine, Measure Up, Brew Deck, Reframe, LiftSafe, Gather.
- Mid tier: Side Boss, The Upcard, Game Shelf, Haunt Inc, All Thumbs, Pigment, Spectra.
- Small by design: Brick Breaker, Sudoku, Five Letters, Jigsaw, Star Stuff, SHARP, HARP, Bloom, Production Triangle.

All three tiers get the same tile. The guides agree on the shape: three or four deep in the foreground, the rest on the shelf as texture. Breadth proves you build a lot; the widget proves you build well. Do not prune. Rank.

Where to spend build time if you want balance: not another small game. Finish the flagships you already started (Nazca's last phase, All Thumbs chapters 8 to 10, SHARP/HARP songbooks, Knight School tiers 2 to 6). Half-done flagships read as half-done; small toys read as toys.

## 6. What other people are building

- OS-style portfolios are now a template genre: Framer sells a macOS portfolio template, Bolt publishes "rebuild a macOS-style portfolio" posts, Fiverr gigs offer them. Your phone shell is in that family. The difference is that theirs open a fake Finder window with a resume in it; yours open 26 working apps. The metaphor is no longer the novelty. The content is.
- Vibe-coded portfolio conventions (from a 20-site roundup): the site is live and is itself the evidence; distinct personal aesthetic (OS-style and "personal internet space" are the two common looks); a playground section for experiments; public repos; documented builds covering what broke and which decisions the AI could not make. You meet all of these except the playground (your lab/ folder is hidden) and the repo link (none on the site).
- neal.fun, the reference "pile of toys" site: a grid of doors, each with a one-glance promise built on a single verb (scroll, draw, spend), information delivered through interaction not explanation, varied styles that read as one curator, restraint in copy. Your verb buttons are the same instinct. Your explanation stack is the opposite instinct.
- "Built on a phone" is no longer unique. A founder shipped a life-admin app on Product Hunt built entirely on an Android phone with an AI agent. The angle still works, but alone it is not a moat. Your moat is range plus your own art plus real recipients plus the family origin story.
- How strangers read AI-built work: disclosing machine authorship cut trust sharply in 13 experiments even at equal quality, while human help barely moved it. Translation for this site: say what you did, give one checkable number per piece, put the tool at the end of the sentence, be ready to explain any build without it open.

## 8. Direction options

Option 1: Presentation session (one session, no new apps). Widget for the flagship, copy reorder, five taglines out, source links, LinkedIn back, desktop caption and card-beside-phone, the 13 defects above. This changes what a stranger sees in seven seconds without changing anything you decided.

Option 2: Video session. Per-app loops cut in RoughCut, dropped into the card galleries. Your profession, finally on your site, and personal work in the sense you meant.

Option 3: Depth session. Pick one flagship and finish its open phase (Nazca's last roadmap phase, or Knight School tier 2) so the strongest cards stop saying "planned."

Recommended order: 1, then 2, then 3. Option 1 is the only one that changes the read of everything already there.
